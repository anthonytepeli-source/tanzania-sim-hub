Interactive science simulations, free and open to everyone.

The Tanzania Simulation Hub offers a growing library of hands-on simulations in Physics, Chemistry, Mathematics, and Astronomy & Astrophysics. Each simulation lets you change the variables yourself, watch the system respond in real time, and build intuition that words alone cannot give. Every simulation is designed to work offline on any phone, tablet, or computer — no data bundle, no sign-up, no cost.

Built for anyone curious about how the physical world works — school students, university students, teachers, and researchers alike. Wherever you are and whatever level you are at, the aim is the same: to make the abstract visible and the invisible touchable.

About the Founders

Built by Tanzanian physicists, for learners everywhere.

The Tanzania Simulation Hub was created by two physicists who met at Tanzanian public universities and shared a single conviction: that interactive, visual learning should not be a privilege reserved for those with fast internet and expensive textbooks.

A.T.
SimHub TZ

Anthony A. Tepeli

Physics student · University of Dar es Salaam

Anthony leads the technical development of the platform — designing and coding every simulation, structuring the hub, and building the offline-first architecture that allows it to run on any shared phone or school computer, wherever it is needed.

His work is grounded in the belief that abstract ideas should become visible, and invisible forces should become touchable, for every learner regardless of where they study.

L.S.
SimHub TZ

Loema Sanzu

Physics alumna · University of Dodoma

Loema leads scientific review and educational design — ensuring every simulation is physically accurate, clearly explained, and pitched so that it serves beginners and advanced learners alike, from first exposure to a topic through to deeper exploration.

Her contribution keeps the hub anchored to real practice, from the classroom to the laboratory, for anyone who wants to understand how the world works.

Physics is not a list of formulas to memorise. It is a way of seeing the world. If a learner anywhere can watch a projectile curve through the air on their own phone and understand why, then this project has done its work.
